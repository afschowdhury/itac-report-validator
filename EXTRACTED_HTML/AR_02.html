Observation and Analysis

During the assessment tour, the LSU-ITAC team found that some of the lighting fixtures in some of the processing buildings were on throughout the day, even when there was enough daylight through the skylights. The team also observed that outdoor lighting fixtures remained operational during daylight hours. In addition, the illumination, measured using Extech® Light Meter, was utilized in assessing the luminance of such buildings. For indoor areas, especially those with skylights, installing daylight sensors will regulate the number of fixtures based on the amount of natural light available. For outdoor lighting, installing photocell sensors will automatically control the operation based on ambient light levels, turning them off during daylight hours and on during darker periods. These automated controls will help prevent unnecessary electrical energy consumption and optimize the facility's lighting operation. A typical daylight sensor and photocell sensor are shown in Figure ‎4-4.

Figure ‎4-4. Daylight Sensor and Photocell Sensor

Recommendation

LSU-ITAC recommends turning off the lights to reduce electricity usage when there is enough daylight. By turning off the lights, the facility will save energy since the lighting fixtures consumed large amounts of power.

Current Energy Usage

The amount of current annual energy usage can be calculated using the following equation:

EUC = W × CF × LH

= 363,400 W × 0.001 kW/W × 6,552 hrs/yr

= 2,380,997 kWh/yr

where

EUC = Current Annual Energy Usage, kWh/yr

W = Total Wattage of Lights

CF = Conversion Factor (kW/W) = 0.001

LH = Lighting Hours = 6,552 hrs/yr

Proposed Energy Usage

The proposed annual electricity usage can be calculated as follows:

EUP = W × CF × LH × RF

= 363,400 W × 0.001 kW/W × 6,552 hrs/yr × 0.7

= 1,666,698 kWh/yr,

where

EUP = Proposed Annual Energy Usage, kWh/yr

W = Total Wattage of Lights

CF = Conversion Factor (kW/W) = 0.001

LH = Lighting Hours = 6,552 hrs/yr

RF = Reduction Factor due to Daylight and Photocell Sensors

Electrical Energy Savings

The total amount of electrical energy savings for this implementation can be calculated as:

ER = EUC – EUP

= 2,380,997 kWh/yr – 1,666,698 kWh/yr

= 714,299 kWh/yr,

where

ER = Energy Reduction, kWh/yr

EUC = Total Current Annual Energy Usage, kWh/yr

EUP = Proposed Annual Energy Usage, kWh/yr

Electrical Energy Cost Savings

The total energy cost savings can be calculated using:

ECS = ER × UR

= 714,299 kWh/yr × $0.082/kWh

= $58,573/yr,

where

ECS = Total Energy Cost Savings, $/yr

ER = Energy Reduction, kWh/yr

UR = Average Electrical Energy Usage Rate = $0.082/kWh

Total Cost Savings

In this case, the total cost savings are the electrical energy cost savings:

TCS = ECS

= $58,573/yr,

where

TCS = Total Cost Savings

ECS = Total Electrical Energy Cost Savings, $/yr

Carbon Dioxide Reduction

Due to the reduced electrical usage, there will also be lower carbon dioxide emissions from the electric power generation. These reductions can be calculated using:

CO2 = ER × K1 × K2

= 714,299 kWh/yr × (0.763 lb. CO2 / kWh) × 1 Ton/2,000lb

= 273 Ton/yr,

where

CO2 = Amount of Carbon Dioxide Reduced, Tons CO2/yr

ER = Electrical Energy Reduction, kWh/yr

K1 = Amount of CO2 Produced per kWh = 0.763 lb. CO2/kWh

K2 = Conversion Factor = 1 ton/2,000 lbs

Implementation Cost

Implementation will involve the cost of daylight and the cost of installation. It is estimated that the average cost of a daylight sensor is $1304F,,. We approximate that the indoor lighting zones can be controlled by 28 of these daylight sensors. The same way the facility would need 14 photocell sensors to control the outdoor lights. The average cost of the photocell sensor is $12 . The installation cost is $25/hr for both and it takes 1 hour to install each sensor. Also, Entergy has a rebate program6F available in daylight sensor installation. According to the prescriptive incentive rates requirement, the facility qualifies as a large commercial entity since its monthly peak demand exceeds 100 kW. The incentive rate is $0.11/W of light covered by sensors. Entergy only gives incentives on whichever is lower among material cost and electrical energy cost savings. The total implementation cost can be estimated by:

IC = (DC × DN – IO × WD) + (PC × PN) + LR × LT

= ($130 × 28 – $0.11/W × 286,000 W) + ($12 × 14) + $25/hour × 42 hours

= $0 (material DC) + $168 (material PC) + $1,050 (labor)

= $1,218

where

IC = Implementation Cost,

DC = Cost per Daylight Sensor, $

PC = Cost per Photocell Sensor, $

DN = Number of Daylight Sensors Needed,

PN = Number of Photocell Sensors Needed,

IO = Incentive Rate for Daylight Sensor Installation, $0.11/W

WD = Collective Wattage for Daylight Sensor Installment, W

LR = Labor Rate, $/hour

LT = Labor Time for Sensor Installation, hour
