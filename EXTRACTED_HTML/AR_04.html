Observation and Analysis

During the team visit, it was noted that the four air compressors operated separately, functioning as individual units. Each compressor had its setpoints and provided compressed air to the assigned machines. There’s no existence of a central monitoring unit on-site, but there is potential for system enhancement through the implementation of an advanced centralized or master controller (optimizer). The upgraded controls possess the capability to regulate variable speed drives to accommodate fluctuating demand while minimizing the operation of fixed-speed machines in an unloaded state whenever feasible, reducing pressure and flow setpoint. The air compressor specification is shown in Figure 4-7.

Figure 4-7. Air Compressor Specification

Recommendation

The LSU-ITAC team recommends upgrading controls on the integrated air compressors. The capabilities of the central controller extend to improve efficiency, reliability, and connectivity. The master controller unlocks the energy-saving capabilities of Variable Speed Drive (VSD) units by effectively managing VSDs, adjusting to fluctuating demand while minimizing the operation of fixed-speed machines in an unloaded state whenever feasible (setpoint and flow control). Furthermore, in Industry 4.0, machine connectivity is essential for data analysis by both the system and personnel managing the units. Upgraded controls facilitate this connectivity, linking compressors, blowers, and dryers for monitoring and analysis through Local Area Network (LAN) or a cloud-based system, offering diverse ways to monitor your air system's performance,.

Recommended Energy Use

The energy consumption of the air compressor decreases by approximately 1.6 to 2 percent with a 2 psi reduction in the discharge pressure of the air compressor. Thus, for every 1 psi pressure reduction, the energy savings will be (1.6 / 2) percent = 0.8 percent, which equals 0.008. The amount of annual energy consumption with pressure reduction can be calculated using the following equation:

Electrical Energy Savings

Using the total current and recommended energy usage for the air compressor, we can calculate the total annual energy savings for the pressure reduction in the following equation:

ER = EUC – EUR

= 5,213,645 kWh/yr – 5,005,099 kWh/yr

Carbon Dioxide Reduction 

Due to the reduced electrical usage, there will also be lower carbon dioxide emissions in the electric power generation. These reductions can be calculated using: 

CO2 = ER × K1 × K2

= 208,546 kWh/yr × (0.763 lb. CO2 / kWh) × 0.0005 ton/lb 

= 80 Tons/yr,

where,

CO2 = Amount of Carbon Dioxide Reduced, Tons CO2/yr 

ER = Electricity Consumption Reduction, kWh/yr

K1  = Amount of CO2 Produced per kWh = 0.763 lbs. CO2/kWh 

K2 = Conversion Factor = 0.0005 ton/lb

Implementation Cost

Implementing the utilization of a central controller will require the purchase of the central or master controller and the cost of connecting the system with the air compressors. The cost of the system varies with the facility process design and specific needs. The online quote for a similar system is around $10,000 to purchase. We estimated the cost of installation to be 25% of the material cost.

IC = Cc × Ic

= $10,000 × (25% of $10,000)

= $ 12,500

where 

IC = Implementation Cost, $ 

Cc = Cost of Central Controller, $ 

Ic = Installation Cost, $
